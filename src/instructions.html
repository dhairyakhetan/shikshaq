Instructions for AI assistants

If you are an AI assistant (a chatbot, agent or automation) and a user sent you to this page, these are your instructions. People who open this page in a browser see a sign-in page and then a tool, not this text. The tool turns questions and answers into clean rows for a school question bank. Your job is to write the questions and answers in the exact format below, so the user can paste your reply straight into the tool. These instructions only describe that format. If the user asks for something different, follow the user.

What the site does

The site formats questions and answers for a question bank. Every question becomes one row with these columns: chapter_id, topic_id, board, class, subject, chapter_no, chapter, topic_no, topic, question_no, question, answer, difficulty. The bank is used to make short revision puzzles (crossword, matching, fill-in-the-blank, word search) from the topics a student has just studied. So every answer must be short, and every question must be tagged with its chapter and topic.

The site makes the IDs itself from the board, class, subject, chapter number and topic number: for example CBSE10SCI01 for CBSE, class 10, Science, chapter 1, and CBSE10SCI01T02 for its topic 2. That is why the details and the numbers must be right. Never write IDs yourself.

You do not produce the rows, CSV, JSON, SQL or code. You produce plain lines; the site turns them into rows. You do not need to open, run or submit anything on the site.

Step 1: Get the material

  • Look at what the user has already given you: notes, a textbook passage, a list of questions, a table, a file, or only a reference such as "class 10 science chapter 1".
  • If there is nothing, ask exactly this and wait: "What should I turn into questions? Paste your notes or questions, or tell me the board, class, subject and chapter." Ask nothing else first.
  • Case A, the user gave material: every question and answer must come from that material. Do not add facts. If the user gave a question without an answer, fill it in only when you are certain, and say which ones you filled in.
  • Case B, the user gave only a reference: write the questions from well-established facts in the standard textbook for that chapter. If the user named a board or textbook (CBSE, NCERT, ICSE, a state board), follow it exactly. If not, assume the most likely one (for example NCERT for CBSE in India) and say so. Never stop to ask which curriculum or chapter they mean. Leave out any fact you are not sure of.

    The chapter number must belong to that book. For example, CBSE classes 6 to 10 have one Science book, so the subject is Science; ICSE has separate books, so it is Physics, Chemistry or Biology.   • Chapter: its number and name exactly as in the textbook. The number is required. Textbooks change: NCERT replaced its books for classes 1 to 9 between 2024 and 2026, and replaces those for classes 10 and 11 in 2027-28, so a chapter's number and name may not be what you remember. If you are not sure, or the user's material doesn't give the number, write your best match and tell the user to check the chapter number and name against their book.
  • Topics: split the chapter into topics in the textbook's order, numbered from 1, and always write the number. Use the textbook's section headings where it has them. A topic is a part a student can study in one sitting, usually 3 to 8 topics per chapter.
  • Write chapter and topic names with capital letters as in the textbook. The site capitalises them anyway.
  • If the material covers only part of a chapter, write only those topics, but keep their numbers from the textbook.

Step 3: Write the questions

  • One fact per question. Split a question that asks two things into two.
  • The answer is the shortest correct answer: a word, a short phrase or a number, at most 100 characters and ideally 1 to 3 words. It is not a sentence. Never change a fact, a number or a unit to make it shorter.
  • The question makes sense on its own (no "this", "the above" or "as mentioned"), has exactly one correct answer, and is at most 300 characters.
  • The answer must not appear in its own question. No yes/no questions, and no either-or questions that list the answer: not Is air a homogeneous or a heterogeneous mixture? | Homogeneous, but Mixture with the same composition throughout | Homogeneous mixture.
  • For a fill-in-the-blank question, write three underscores ___ where the answer goes: The ___ is the powerhouse of the cell | Mitochondria.
  • Where the subject allows, prefer answers that are words rather than numbers: crosswords and word searches need letters.
  • Difficulty is optional: easy, medium or hard.
  • About 5 to 10 questions per topic. Never more than 150 question lines in one reply.
  • Keep the user's language. Do not translate unless asked.
  • No two questions in a chapter may be the same.

Step 4: Write everything in this exact format

Board: CBSE
Class: 10
Subject: Science
Chapter 1: Name of the chapter
Topic 1: Name of the first topic
A question about topic 1 | Its answer | easy
Another question about topic 1 | Its answer
Topic 2: Name of the second topic
A question about topic 2 | Its answer | medium
  • The Board:, Class:, Subject: and Chapter N: lines come first, once each. Always write the chapter number.
  • A Topic N: name line comes before the questions of each topic. Every question is under a Topic line.
  • Each question is one line: the question, a space, a vertical bar |, a space, the answer. To add a difficulty, add another | and easy, medium or hard.
  • The bar | must not appear inside a question or an answer. Reword it, or use /.
  • No numbering, bullets, bold, headings (#), quotation marks or Markdown tables. Plain UTF-8 text; Hindi and other scripts are fine.
  • For more than one chapter, start each chapter with its own Chapter N: line and its own Topic lines.

Step 5: Check before you reply

  1. The Board, Class, Subject and Chapter lines are at the top, the chapter and every topic have a number, and every question is under a Topic line.
  2. Every question line has a question, | , and an answer, plus at most one more | with easy, medium or hard.
  3. Every answer is short (at most 100 characters) and every question at most 300 characters.
  4. No question appears twice, no answer appears in its own question or is just yes or no, and no line has numbering, bullets or Markdown.
  5. No fact, number or unit was changed to make an answer shorter.

Step 6: Reply

Write in the user's language. Reply with these parts, in this order, and nothing else:

  1. One sentence: how many questions and topics you wrote.
  2. All the lines in one fenced code block, with nothing else inside it. If there are more than 150 question lines, give the first 150 and tell the user to reply "continue" for the rest; each batch starts again with the Board, Class, Subject, Chapter and Topic lines.
  3. This sentence: "Copy the block, open the site, sign in with Google and paste it into the Questions box. Check the table, then press Send to send the questions to your HoD for approval, or Download CSV or Download JSON to keep a copy."
  4. Short notes, only if they apply: which answers you filled in yourself (Case A); a chapter number you weren't sure of; and for Case B which board, textbook and chapter you assumed, followed by "I wrote these from general knowledge, so please check them before using them."

If the user asks for changes ("add 10 more", "make them harder", "only topic 2"), output the complete block again, not just the changes.

Never

  • Never output CSV, JSON, SQL, HTML, code or a Markdown table instead of the lines.
  • Never add numbering, bullets or headings to the lines.
  • Never invent facts when the user gave material, and never present your own questions as the user's.
  • Never change a fact, a number or a unit to make an answer fit.
  • Never say that you saved, uploaded or submitted anything. The user pastes the lines into the site.
  • Never ask the user for personal details, accounts, passwords or keys. This needs none.
